IMMUTABLE MICRORTS GAMEPLAY CONTRACT.

Treat this as a closed-world description of the game available to a strategy.
A policy may use any strategic identity, unit composition, timing, positioning,
branching, or opponent adaptation expressible with these mechanics. It may
depart substantially from its parent strategy. Do not assume an entity,
capability, observation, or action that is not listed here.

Game objective and world:
- Two players act on a rectangular grid measured in cells and game cycles.
- The evaluated game state is fully observable; there is no fog-of-war or
  information-revealing scouting mechanic.
- A player wins by eliminating every enemy-owned unit and building. A match
  that reaches its configured cycle limit without a winner is a draw.
- Cells may be blocked by terrain or occupied. Movement, production, and
  building placement require a legal reachable or adjacent cell as applicable.
- Resources are neutral map entities. Each player also has one scalar resource
  stockpile used to pay production and construction costs.

Complete entity set and production graph:
- Resource: neutral, stationary, harvestable, and not a player unit.
- Base: stationary stockpile; costs 10 resources, has 10 HP, and produces Worker.
- Barracks: stationary; costs 5 resources, has 4 HP, and produces Light, Heavy,
  or Ranged units.
- Worker: costs 1, has 1 HP, moves, attacks at range 1, harvests one resource at
  a time, returns carried resources to a friendly Base, and builds Base or
  Barracks.
- Light: costs 2, has 4 HP, moves fastest among the listed combat units, attacks
  at range 1, and deals 2 damage.
- Heavy: costs 2, has 4 HP, moves slower than Light, attacks at range 1, and
  deals 4 damage.
- Ranged: costs 2, has 1 HP, moves, attacks at range 3, and deals 1 damage.
- Base and Barracks do not move, harvest, or attack. Light, Heavy, and Ranged
  units do not harvest, return resources, construct buildings, or produce units.
- Default action durations in game cycles are: Worker move 10, attack 5,
  harvest 20, return 10, and production 50; Light move 8 and attack 5; Heavy
  move 12 and attack 5; Ranged move 10 and attack 5; Base construction 250;
  Barracks construction 200; Light production 80; Heavy production 120; Ranged
  production 100.

Complete strategic action set:
- Assign an action only to an idle friendly unit; one unit executes one action
  at a time and the action completes over game cycles.
- Move a movable unit through orthogonally adjacent legal cells. Competing
  movement or production into the same cell may be cancelled, so avoid
  conflicting destinations.
- Target an enemy-owned unit or building; move toward it when necessary and
  attack once it is within the attacker's range.
- Have a Worker harvest a Resource and return its carried resource to a
  friendly Base.
- Have a Base train a Worker into a free adjacent cell and pay its cost.
- Have a Barracks train Light, Heavy, or Ranged into a free adjacent cell and
  pay its cost.
- Have a Worker build a Base or Barracks on a legal free cell and pay its cost.
- Leave a unit idle when no higher-priority legal action applies.

Canonical form for executable policy rules:
- Every action rule is stand-alone. It names an idle friendly actor and all
  prerequisites in the same rule; it never assumes that an earlier rule has
  completed.
- Write one complete condition/action sentence per numbered rule. Do not add an
  "otherwise" or second-sentence action that reuses "that unit" without again
  naming the idle friendly actor and its complete prerequisites.
- Worker production example: if a friendly Base is idle, the stockpile is at
  least 1, and a free cell adjacent to that Base exists, have that Base train a
  Worker into that cell.
- Combat production example: if a friendly Barracks is idle, the stockpile is
  at least 2, and a free cell adjacent to that Barracks exists, have that
  Barracks train one named combat-unit type into that cell.
- Construction example: if a friendly Worker is idle, the stockpile meets the
  building cost, and a legal free cell adjacent to that Worker exists, have
  that Worker build the named building on that cell.
- Harvest example: if a friendly Worker is idle, carries no resource, and a
  reachable Resource exists, have that Worker harvest the nearest reachable
  Resource.
- Return example: if a friendly Worker is idle, carries a resource, and a
  friendly Base exists, have that Worker return the resource to the nearest
  friendly Base.
- Combat example: if a friendly movable attacker is idle and a current enemy
  entity exists, have the attacker target that entity, move toward it if it is
  outside attack range, and attack it when it enters range.
- A distance or attack-range condition for combat is measured from the acting
  unit named in that rule, not from some other friendly unit or building. Base
  and Barracks have no attack range. Never target or attack a friendly entity.
- An immediate attack rule must state that its target is currently within the
  acting unit's own attack range. If the target may be outside that range, the
  same rule must target it, move toward it, and attack only after it enters the
  actor's range. Split mixed unit types when their legal ranges differ.
- Never target or attack a Resource or combine it with an enemy-target
  fallback. Resource appears only in a Worker's fully guarded harvest rule.

Observable strategy state:
- Current game cycle, player side, player resource stockpile, and map width and
  height.
- Every current entity's type, owner, grid position, carried resource amount,
  current HP, and maximum HP.
- Whether a friendly unit is currently idle and whether a candidate grid cell
  is in bounds and free.
- Relative distance and counts may be derived from those observations.
- Opponent implementation name/class, hidden intent, future actions, wall-clock
  seconds, technology levels, upgrades, and any state not listed above are not
  observable strategy conditions. Translate opponent-specific evidence into
  responses to observable unit, building, economy, position, or timing cues.

Closed-world interpretation rules:
- Fully observable means every currently existing enemy entity is present in
  the current state. Do not invent fog of war, partial visibility, scouting, or
  a distinction between an existing entity and a visible entity.
- Cells have terrain and occupancy, but no owner, territory, starting-cell,
  spawn-location, safety, or capture status. Describe positioning with current
  coordinates, free cells, map bounds, and distances to current entities.
- Current positions do not reveal movement history, velocity, convergence,
  intent, an action target, or whether a unit or building is under attack.
  Express danger with current enemy type, position, attack range, and distance;
  express a response with a listed action such as target, move, or attack.
- Current entities do not reveal when an opponent built or produced them, when
  another entity was destroyed, production trends, unit creation timestamps,
  or whether an economy is stagnant. Test current entity counts/types and the
  current cycle instead of historical events.
- Strategy prompts describe game-level behavior, not Java implementation. They
  must not contain helper names, method calls, variable names, code fragments,
  or API instructions.
- Words such as "immediately" may specify the highest priority for the next
  idle-unit decision; they never imply zero-duration movement, training, or
  construction. A "defensive" strategy is valid, but each defensive response
  must resolve to a listed target, movement, attack, production, build, harvest,
  return, or idle action rather than an undefined defend/block command.

Grounding requirement:
- Every condition in a generated policy must use observable state listed above.
- Every response and fallback must use the listed entities and legal actions.
- Before returning a policy or diagnosis, check every stated rule against this
  contract and reformulate any rule that cannot be executed in this closed world.
